# The playbook audit found two dead controls, and building its documents found a legal deadline that was invented

**When:** 2026-09-02T09:15:53Z · **Branch:** `claude/session-handoff-20260902` (work landed from six branches) · **PR:** #2165, #2166, #2167, #2168, #2169, #2170 — all merged · **Phase 0:** `RESULT: green (with skips: tests)`, log `.handoff-logs/handoff-20260902T091553Z.log`

## 1. Summary of what was done

Six pull requests merged. The session began as an audit of RestoreAssist against
the AI-Native SDLC Playbook and ended having fixed three live defects that had
nothing to do with the playbook.

**The playbook adoption (#2165, #2166).** The repository was already further into
the playbook than the enterprises it was written for — 62 skills, 35 plans, 34
specs, a two-stage Stop-hook verifier, 24 workflows. Stages 3 and 4 were largely
done. Missing were Stage 1 (`intent/`), Stage 4's config coverage (`evals/`),
Stage 5 (`REVIEW.md` and a multi-model review panel), and Stage 6
(`scripts/observability/`).

**Both `PreToolUse` guards had never worked.** They read `$CLAUDE_TOOL_INPUT`
and `$CLAUDE_FILE_PATH`, which Claude Code does not set — the payload arrives as
JSON on stdin — and exited 1 on a match, which is a non-blocking hook error. For
as long as they existed they printed `BLOCKED: destructive command detected` and
let the command run. Two independent defects in a control everyone believed was
holding. Proven dead before being fixed, by extracting the configured commands
out of the settings file and feeding them the documented payload. They also
lived in `settings.local.json`, which Claude Code gitignores by convention, so
the next person following that convention would have dropped them silently.

**`leadRisk` was derived from the asbestos year.** Every Australian building
between the lead and asbestos presumption years was reported as a lead risk on an
asbestos ban's authority. Found while landing the Australian lead registry entry,
which had itself been blocked on an edition-year contradiction (2014 and 2016 are
two printings of the same 5th edition, not a conflict).

**Five of spec section 9.3's eight documents built (#2167, #2168, #2169,
#2170).** Asbestos Assessment Authority, Silica Risk Control Plan, WHS Site
Induction Record, Certificate of Completion and Notifiable Incident Record joined
the two that already existed (Authority to Commence Works, and the SWMS in
`lib/swms/auto-generator.ts`). **Seven of the eight now exist**; the Client
Review / Satisfaction Form was not reached — see section 7. All ten templates in
the catalogue are now offered automatically from the report.

**The notification deadline was invented.** `safework-notification-gate.ts` told
contractors they had **24 hours** to notify the regulator after a serious
incident, counted from the **inspection date**, under a comment reading
`// per WHS Act`. Australia requires notification IMMEDIATELY (model WHS Act
s38); New Zealand AS SOON AS POSSIBLE (HSWA s56). The figure 24 appears in
neither Act — the only 48 hours in the WHS Act is a written follow-up conditional
on the regulator asking. And the duty runs from becoming aware, so an incident
found a week after the inspection was handed a deadline already in the past.

## 3. Decisions locked + what shipped

**Independence is counted by model family, not by seat.** Two checkpoints of one
base model share their blind spots, so the two Nemotron seats are one vote. The
panel reports "1 family only — not corroborated" rather than presenting a single
opinion as consensus.

**The detector refuses to give a band position.** The verifier corpus spans 116
days but holds six active days across four sessions, with 225 of 379 reports on
one day. A mean and sigma over that describes when somebody happened to be
working. `verifier-metrics.ts` returns `insufficient-data` below eight populated
windows and names its sample size.

**Cost is structural, not budgeted.** Three of four panel seats are OpenRouter
`:free` variants, which cannot bill. The fourth is MiniMax-M2.5 at about 1.5 US
cents per PR. Codex and Claude Code appear only as *local* seats, on
subscriptions already held — OpenAI's guidance is that a plan is licensed for
interactive use and automation should hold its own credential.

**Two citation forms in the document catalogue, and the difference is
load-bearing.** An exact registry id says "this duty exists in one country". A
*family* (`{domain, rule}`) resolves to the job's country and, when it resolves
to nothing, says "no verified entry is held here" — which is not the same as "no
duty exists". Citing RCD protection by exact id would print an Australian
regulation on a New Zealand induction under a notice a reader could fairly take
as "New Zealand has no RCD duty".

**A third form for IICRC standards**, because they are a second source of truth
with a sharper jurisdiction problem: on an Australian job the governing document
is `AS-IICRC S500:2025`, a *modified* adoption whose changes sit in Appendix ZZ,
so citing the ANSI original omits requirements. No clause is cited at all —
`standardCite()` numbers against the ANSI edition, and an Australian job is not
governed by that document.

**The notification countdown was removed, not corrected.** A timer tells a reader
they have time in hand, and they do not.

**Suggestions err toward offering; content errs toward disclosing.** A form
offered and declined costs a click; a hazard document not offered costs the thing
it was written to prevent. So an unrecorded building year still offers the
asbestos authority, one priority lower, naming what was missing. That is the
inverse of the rule for regulatory content, and both refuse to let an unknown
pass as a known.

## 7. Deferred + open questions

1. **`OPENROUTER_API_KEY` / `MINIMAX_API_KEY` as repository secrets.** Owner
   decision. Without them the panel skips and nothing else is affected.
2. **May the panel comment on PRs**, or write a check-run summary only at first?
   It can neither approve nor block either way.
3. **The `bands.yaml` trigger layer stays unwired.** It needs a scheduled job
   with model access (rule 31), and its `propose` tier would let an agent open
   pull requests unprompted. Both are the owner's to authorise.
4. **`REGULATOR_MAP` still holds nine regulator names and URLs outside the
   registry.** A directory rather than a rule; moving it touches the
   notification path more widely than one change should.
5. **The 2025 model WHS Act amendments** (work-related suicide, violent
   incidents, mobile plant and falls, 15+ day absences) are recorded as *may
   apply, confirm with your regulator*. Per-state adoption was not researched.
   Doing so would let the entries state the position per jurisdiction.
6. **`lib/compliance/nzbs-compliance-gate.ts` is still inert.** `propertyCountry`
   exists on `Inspection` and the stale TODOs claiming otherwise were corrected,
   but the caller does not thread it through. Un-inerting it starts **blocking**
   New Zealand submissions that currently pass, so it wants its own change set.
7. **Client Review / Satisfaction Form** is the one section 9.3 document not
   built. It cites nothing regulatory, so it was never blocked — just not
   reached.
8. **A false positive in the destructive-command guard, found writing this
   document.** The guard fired on a `cat > file` heredoc whose body merely
   described why the test gate skips. A heredoc fed to `cat` is data, and
   `strip_heredocs()` should have removed it before the pattern scan. The guard
   is doing its job in the safe direction, but this is the second false positive
   of the same shape (the first matched `--argjson` as an `rm -r` flag) and it
   deserves a regression case in `.claude/hooks/tests/test-guards.sh`.
